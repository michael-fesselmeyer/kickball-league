# NYC Parks athletic field permits

What the league needs to know to get field time from NYC Parks, and how the capacity planner turns that field time into the number of teams a season can take. The owners' permit screen shows a plain-language version of this page ("How NYC Parks permits work"); keep the two in step.

Every fact below links to the page it came from. All pages were read on **2026-11-14** unless another date is given. NYC Parks changed its athletic permit rules and fees on November 1, 2024 ([help page](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/contact)); check the links again before each application period.

## How and where to apply

- Organized leagues need a permit to reserve a field; the online **Field and Court Requests** system takes the application after creating an account ([Request](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request)).
- Applications go to the borough permit office where the field is, by mail, through the website or in person; each borough is a separate application, and online requests must come in at least 7 days before the first requested date ([Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (b)(2)).
- An **adult** permit is for participants over 18 who aren't in a school program ([Priorities](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/priorities)). Rule 2-12 counts a scheduled, officiated league game with recorded results as a "Game"; pick-up play is "Practice" ([Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (a)).
- A league can register as an **Organization**; review takes up to two weeks and may ask for the website, bylaws, rosters and schedule. Permit requests should be ready to send schedules and rosters ([Priorities](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/priorities)).
- Kickball is one of the sports the availability map filters by, and McCarren Park's map shows what is permitted and what is free this season, with a spreadsheet of issued permits ([McCarren permits](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/issued/B058)).

The rule is the binding text; the web page may reflect current practice. Ask the borough office if it matters.

## Why the fall has less room

- **Multi-use fields change sports.** On fields built for several sports, spring and summer priority goes to bat-and-ball sports (baseball, softball, cricket); fall and winter priority goes to sports on rectangular fields (football, lacrosse, soccer). Out-of-season requests are considered only if no qualified priority applicant wants the field, and an out-of-season permit doesn't make the league a returning applicant the next year ([Priorities](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/priorities); [Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (b)(4)).
- **Youth first.** In each application period, youth game requests are handled before any adult game requests ([Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (b)(6)–(7)), and school leagues can get priority on weekdays from 3 to 6 pm ([Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (c)(1)).
- **Maintenance and rest.** Parks can leave field time unpermitted for maintenance or other uses ([Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (c)(1)), and no grass ballfields are available from December 1 through March 16 ([Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (b)(4)).
- **Daylight.** New York's sunset is 7:28 pm on September 1, 6:39 pm on September 30 and 4:30 pm on November 28 ([Sun](https://www.timeanddate.com/sun/usa/new-york?month=9&year=2026)), so fall weeknight games need a lit field. Only 330 of the 6,951 athletic facilities in NYC Parks' dataset are lit ([Athletic Facilities](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/dataset/Athletic-Facilities/qnem-b8re), read through its free API), and bringing portable lights to a field costs the permit ([Guidelines](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/rules)).

## Returning permittees

- A **returning applicant** is a youth or adult league listed as active in NYC Parks' athletic organization database asking for the same number of hours and fields at a location as it held the previous year or season. Asking for different hours or more fields makes the change a new request ([Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (a)).
- A returning adult league that complied with last season's permit and Parks' rules gets preference for the same dates and times if it asks during the renewal or new-request periods. Permits issued after those periods can't be renewed ([Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (b)(7)).
- One holder may renew at most 50 hours a week; where fields are in high demand Parks can grant part of a request or another location ([Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (c)(10)).
- After the season starts, requests are handled first come, first served ([Priorities](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/priorities)).

For the league this means: keep asking for the same hours every season to stay "returning", and ask for extra hours as a separate new request, which is what the capacity planner's request links produce.

## Fees for adult leagues

Per hour, from the [Priorities](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/priorities) page:

| Facility | Adult (18 and over) |
| --- | --- |
| Synthetic turf, large or full field | $41 |
| Synthetic turf, multipurpose | $28 |
| Natural grass | $24 |
| Hard top | $20 |
| Field lights (added to the field fee) | $15 |

Fees are due 14 days before the season starts, or within 3 business days for permits granted later ([Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (c)(13)). Permits are free for people under 18 ([McCarren diamonds](https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/mccarren-park/facilities/baseball)). No application or processing fee is listed on these pages.

**What the app does with this** ([ADR 0055](../adr/0055-permit-fees-hours-and-give-back.md)): until an owner enters what Parks charged, the permits screen estimates each permit at these rates. It counts the permit's hours at the field's surface rate. The surface comes from NYC Open Data's field list: the field at the league's park with the same name, or the surface every kickball field there shares, or natural grass when nothing matches. It adds the lights fee for each hour after sunset, worked out in the app for the park's location. The books show the estimates as expected permit fees, apart from what has been spent, with the due date 14 days before the season starts. Owners are emailed 30 and 7 days before it.

## Insurance

An applicant with more than 50 permitted hours in a season needs commercial general liability insurance for the whole permit, in the organization's and applicant's name, of at least $1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 in aggregate, naming the City of New York with its officers, directors and employees as additional insureds. It sends a certificate of insurance and a notarized [broker's certification form](https://www.nycgovparks.org/sub_about/parks_divisions/concessions/pdfs_and_docs/insurance_broker_certification.pdf); the portal blocks further applications until they are approved ([Priorities](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/priorities); [Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (c)(5)). Parks may also ask for a clean-up bond ([Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (c)(6)).

The permits screen counts the season's permitted hours (requested and confirmed, not lost) against the 50-hour line. It warns from 40 hours, and the request sheet warns when the hours it asks for would cross the line. Owners are emailed once when a season reaches 40 hours and once when it passes 50.

## At the field

- Each group must have its permit at the field; a digital copy counts ([Guidelines](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/rules); [Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (c)(7)). The app keeps it on the ref's and captains' phones.
- The permit holder answers for its players and spectators. Permits can't be transferred or resold, and Parks can end, suspend or move a permit ([Guidelines](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/rules)).
- **Alcohol** may not be sold or consumed on Parks property unless the Commissioner allows it ([Guidelines](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/rules); [Rule 1-05](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/newyorkcity/latest/NYCrules/0-0-0-87799), (f)), and glass bottles are banned ([Rule 1-05](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/newyorkcity/latest/NYCrules/0-0-0-87799), (d)).
- **Amplified sound** needs a permit ([Rule 1-05](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/newyorkcity/latest/NYCrules/0-0-0-87799), (d)(2)): an NYPD Sound Device Application filed in person at the local precinct at least five days ahead, with a $45 fee; Parks allows at most four hours of amplified sound and not in every park ([Events guide](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/special-events/guide)).
- No selling without a separate Temporary Use Authorization; no barbecues or propane; no litter; one banner on the fence during the permit, taken down after ([Guidelines](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/rules)).
- No portable lights, and no goals, mounds, toilets or equipment left at the field without written permission ([Guidelines](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/rules)).
- **Enforcement:** a permit not used can be cut back or ended, and breaking the rules can cost the league all its permits or bring a summons ([Guidelines](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/rules); [Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (c)(9) and (c)(11)). Anyone can report a field being misused or a permit going unused through 311, under "Obstructing Public Use" ([Request](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request)). The request page says Parks Enforcement Patrol staffs its late-night open-play pilot; it does not describe routine checks of league permits.

## Weather, closures and refunds

- Parks can cancel permitted play for bad weather or conditions that could damage the field, and issues no credits or refunds ([Rule 2-12](https://www.nycgovparks.org/rules/section-2-12), (c)(3)). There are no refunds or credits for unused permit time, whatever the reason ([Guidelines](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/rules)).
- If the league can't use a date, call the borough permit office ahead so the time can go to someone else ([Guidelines](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request/rules)). There is an online [Athletic Permit Cancellation Request](https://forms.office.com/Pages/ResponsePage.aspx?id=x2_1MoFfIk6pWxXaZlE7710LKJS3C2ZGtGmBgW1cyQFUNzJJRkoxR0pNUk02MFJUSE00STlKSFpBRi4u) form, linked from the [Request](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/request) page. It asks for the 6-digit permit number, the permit holder's name, the date to cancel, the borough (BK, BX, MN, QS or SI), and the park, field number and reason.
- When a rain-out cancels every slot in a permit window, or a blackout drops its date, the permits screen lists that window if it hasn't started yet. Each listing has the borough office's number and the form's answers ready to copy, and owners are emailed once for each window. A permit left unused can be cut back, which costs the league its returning status (see "Enforcement" above).
- Field closures are posted on [NYC Parks notices](https://www.nycgovparks.org/news/notices). We found no NYC Parks page with a day-of rain-out line for ballfields; the league's own rain-out call (the app's weather alerts) stays the way players hear about it.

## McCarren Park's fields

- McCarren Park (park ID B058) has three grass baseball fields (one between Bedford and Driggs avenues west of Lorimer Street, two on North 12th Street between Bedford and Driggs) and two asphalt fields at North 12th Street and Bedford Avenue ([McCarren diamonds](https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/mccarren-park/facilities/baseball)).
- Its synthetic full-size soccer field is lit and open 8 am to 10 pm, for soccer year-round ([McCarren soccer](https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/mccarren-park/facilities/soccer)).
- NYC Parks' dataset marks all three grass diamonds as permitting kickball (two also for adult softball, one for adult baseball); only that baseball diamond is lit ([Athletic Facilities](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/dataset/Athletic-Facilities/qnem-b8re)).

## The capacity planner

Owners open it from the permit screen (`/app/books/<season>/permits/capacity`). It reads the season's permit windows (not the lost ones), blackout dates, the teams in the season and the waitlist, and works out:

1. **Capacity.** Each window holds `floor(window minutes ÷ game length)` back-to-back games. A season fits `n` teams when, summing over game days, `min(slots that day, floor(n × games a team plays per day ÷ 2))` reaches `ceil(n × games per team ÷ 2)`. The planner checks every count up to 200, because an odd count can fail where the next even one fits (one team sits out each day). It is a bound the scheduler can meet, not a schedule.
2. **The gap** to the teams that want to play: teams in the season plus teams waiting (waitlisted entries and captains on the waitlist), and how many games short the season is for them.
3. **Options,** each on its own with the teams it fits: games 5, 10 or 15 minutes shorter; doubleheaders; a free weeknight (6:30–9:30 pm, needing lights in the fall); weekend daytime (10 am–4 pm); one more field at the usual times; two divisions on different nights (each division fits on its own); and two fewer games per team.
4. **Exactly the extra hours.** Options that add field time spread the games the season is short over the new dates, never more a day than the new window holds or the waiting teams can play, and link to the request sheet with those games per day filled in. The sheet turns them into the dates, field and hours to paste into the NYC Parks application.
5. **Nearby fields.** NYC Parks diamonds and large multi-use fields on natural grass, synthetic turf or clay within 5 km (about 3 miles) of the league's parks, nearest first, with surface, lights and whether NYC Parks permits kickball there. The data is a snapshot of NYC Open Data's Athletic Facilities dataset (with park names from [Parks Properties, enfh-gkve](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/dataset/Parks-Properties/enfh-gkve)), checked in at `src/domain/capacity/nyc-park-fields.json` with its source and fetch date. Refresh it with `npx tsx scripts/fetch-park-fields.ts`; the free API needs no key at this volume ([Socrata app tokens](https://dev.socrata.com/docs/app-tokens.html)). The app never calls the API at request time.

Nothing is sent to NYC Parks; owners copy the sheet into the application themselves.

## Watching for openings: not built

NYC Parks publishes a spreadsheet of each park's issued permits for the current season ([McCarren permits](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/field-and-court/issued/B058), "download a spreadsheet"). A daily check of it during the first-come periods could tell owners when kickball-suitable time looks open. The site's [robots.txt](https://www.nycgovparks.org/robots.txt) doesn't disallow that path, and the [nyc.gov terms of use](https://www.nyc.gov/main/terms-of-use) say nothing against automated reading. But the site answers automated requests with 403 Forbidden, the spreadsheet included, even though it opens in a browser (checked 2026-11-14). Getting past that would mean posing as a browser to get around the site's own refusal, so the app doesn't do it ([ADR 0055](../adr/0055-permit-fees-hours-and-give-back.md)). Owners can open the park's map and spreadsheet from the permits screen themselves. NYC Open Data has no dataset of issued permits; its [Athletic Facilities](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/dataset/Athletic-Facilities/qnem-b8re) list says where fields are, not when they are free.
